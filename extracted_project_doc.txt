w14:paraId="79979323">ABSTRACT
w14:paraId="39639394">The AI-Integrated Construction Project and Resource Management System is a web-based application developed to improve the management of construction projects, resources, procurement activities, and financial operations. The system integrates project management, employee management, material tracking, reporting, and financial monitoring into a single platform to enhance efficiency and coordination. It also incorporates Artificial Intelligence technologies to provide project cost prediction, delay prediction, resource optimization, and decision-support services. The system is designed using a three-tier architecture with secure data management and role-based access control to ensure reliability and security. By automating key construction management processes and providing intelligent insights, the proposed solution improves project performance, optimizes resource utilization, supports informed decision-making, and reduces delays and operational challenges in construction management.

w14:paraId="1578615F">Chapter 1: Introduction
w14:paraId="36A99E70">Background of the Project
w14:paraId="4643DB76">The construction industry is one of the most important sectors contributing to economic growth and infrastructure development in Ethiopia. Construction companies are involved in building residential apartments, commercial buildings, schools, hospitals, roads, and other public infrastructures. Managing construction projects requires efficient coordination of workers, materials, suppliers, expenses, and project schedules.
w14:paraId="55C8318A">Despite the growth of the construction sector in Ethiopia, many construction companies still rely on manual and traditional management methods. Worker attendance is often recorded using notebooks, material inventory is tracked manually, supplier communication is conducted through phone calls, and project expenses are documented using paper-based records or simple spreadsheets. These manual processes are time-consuming, prone to human error, and make it difficult for managers to access real-time information.
w14:paraId="1A1764A2">As construction projects become larger and more complex, manual management creates several challenges, including poor workforce coordination, material shortages, budget overruns, project delays, and lack of visibility into project progress. Furthermore, most companies do not utilize historical project data to support decision-making, making it difficult to predict future costs, resource requirements, or potential delays.
w14:paraId="40D7CDEF">Recent advancements in web technologies and Artificial Intelligence (AI) provide opportunities to improve construction management practices. AI can analyze project data, predict project costs, forecast material requirements, detect delay risks, and recommend resource allocation strategies.
w14:paraId="01AF6B2D">Therefore, this project proposes the development of an AI-Integrated Construction Project and Resource Management System that digitizes project management, worker management, material management, supplier management, expense tracking, and reporting processes. The system aims to improve efficiency, transparency, decision-making, and overall project performance in Ethiopian construction companies.
w14:paraId="2BF32E7C">Problems and Justifications
w14:paraId="5408DD28">Problem StatementCurrently, many construction companies in Ethiopia face significant operational challenges due to the use of manual and fragmented management systems.Manual Record KeepingWorker information, attendance records, material inventories, and expenses are commonly maintained using notebooks, paper documents, or spreadsheets. This
w14:paraId="23783B6C">approach increases the risk of data loss, duplication, and human errors.Poor Material ManagementConstruction materials such as cement, steel, sand, and aggregates are often tracked manually. As a result, companies experience material shortages, overstocking, wastage, and difficulties in monitoring inventory levels.Weak Workforce ManagementMany companies lack a centralized system for managing workers. Attendance tracking, task assignment, and productivity monitoring are performed manually, making it difficult to evaluate workforce performance and resource utilization.Inefficient Supplier CoordinationMaterial orders are frequently placed through phone calls or informal communication channels. There is often no proper mechanism for tracking supplier orders, delivery schedules, or supplier performance.Lack of Cost and Budget ControlProject expenses are usually recorded manually, making it difficult to compare actual spending against planned budgets. This often contributes to budget overruns and financial inefficiencies.Limited Project VisibilityManagers are unable to monitor project progress in real time. Project delays are often identified late, reducing the ability to take corrective actions promptly.Absence of Data-Driven Decision MakingMost construction companies do not utilize project data for predictive analysis. Consequently, they cannot accurately forecast project costs, material requirements, workforce needs, or delay risks.
